Flood risk, grouped by the shape of the land.

FloodClusterMST splits a terrain into planning regions so that each region holds land that floods alike. It is a graph problem, solved with algorithms written from scratch.

1. Build a graph

Every grid cell is a location, connected to its 4 or 8 neighbours. An edge costs a weighted mix of the elevation change, the difference in flood risk and the distance between the two cells. The sliders set those weights.

2. Find the cheapest tree

Kruskal's algorithm with a union-find structure keeps the 399 cheapest edges that connect all 400 cells without a cycle: the minimum spanning tree.

3. Cut where it's steepest

Removing the k−1 most expensive edges of that tree leaves k regions. The cuts fall where land and risk change the most, which is where a planner would draw a border.

The sample terrain is synthetic and seeded. The algorithms are verified against networkx on random graphs in the repository's test suite. Built by Mostafa Amer and Faahil Ali.
